Reasoning in Artificial Intelligence

Reasoning GATE DA 📖 Topic Overview

Reasoning is the process by which an AI agent derives new conclusions from existing knowledge. It is the core mechanism that allows an intelligent system to go beyond what it has been directly told and infer new facts automatically.

Simple Definition: Reasoning = using what you know to figure out what you don't know. Given a set of facts and rules, reasoning produces new facts that must logically follow.

What is Reasoning?

In AI, reasoning works on top of a Knowledge Base (KB). The KB contains facts and rules. The reasoning engine applies inference rules to derive new conclusions. 

  Knowledge Base:
  ├── Fact:  "It is raining"              (P)
  └── Rule:  "If it rains → roads are wet" (P → Q)

  Reasoning derives:
  └── New Fact: "Roads are wet"           (Q)  ✓

Two Key Properties of Good Reasoning

PropertyMeaning
Soundness Only derives conclusions that are actually true — no wrong answers
Completeness Derives every conclusion that logically follows — misses nothing

Types of Reasoning in AI

TypeDirectionDescriptionExample
Deductive General → Specific Derives specific facts from general rules. Guaranteed to be correct. All men are mortal. Socrates is a man. → Socrates is mortal.
Inductive Specific → General Derives general rules from specific observations. Not guaranteed. The sun rose every day so far → The sun always rises.
Abductive Observation → Best Explanation Finds the simplest explanation for an observation. The road is wet → It probably rained.
GATE Focus: The GATE DA syllabus focuses primarily on Deductive Reasoning — specifically logical inference using Forward Chaining, Backward Chaining, and Resolution.

GATE Tips

  • Deductive reasoning is the main type tested in GATE — always valid if premises are true.
  • Sound = never wrong. Complete = never misses anything.
  • An inference algorithm should be both sound and complete for reliable reasoning.
  • Forward Chaining starts from facts and applies rules to derive new facts.
  • Backward Chaining starts from the goal and works backward to find supporting facts.
  • Resolution is the key inference rule used in automated theorem proving.
  • Reasoning is the engine that makes logic useful in practice — logic is the language, reasoning is the thinking.
